What a Professional Discussion is

A Professional Discussion is a planned conversation with your assessor. Instead of writing an assignment, you talk through a scenario and explain what you would do and why. Your assessor asks follow-up questions to check your understanding and to hear how you apply it in practice.

It is not a test of memory. Your assessor wants to hear that you can recognise a situation, act within your role, involve the right people, and explain the reasons behind your actions.

The PE criteria ask you to evaluate. That means going beyond describing something: say what works well, what the limits are, and what you would do differently.

How it runs

  1. You get a scenario. Take time to read it and jot down a few notes on what stands out.
  2. You talk it through. Explain what you notice, what you would do, who you would involve and why.
  3. Your assessor asks follow-up questions. These help you show more of what you know, so treat them as a second chance, not a trap.
  4. Your assessor records the outcome against each AC on the marking sheet.
Your tutor will confirm the date, length and format (online or in person) of each discussion.

How to answer well

Use a simple structure

For safeguarding scenarios, try Recognise, Respond, Record, Report, Reflect. For health promotion, try What is it, Why does it matter, What does the evidence say, How well does it work.

Stay within your role

Be clear about what a dental nurse does and when you would pass something on to the dentist, the safeguarding lead or an outside service.

Name the guidance

Mention the legislation and guidance behind your answer, such as GDC Standards for the Dental Team, the Children Act, the Care Act 2014 or Delivering Better Oral Health. You don't need to quote them word for word, but show you know why they matter.

Use real examples

Where you can, talk about your own practice: your safeguarding lead, your policies, an oral health session you have seen or helped with. Keep patient details anonymous.

Evaluate, don't just describe

For the PE criteria, weigh things up. What are the strengths? What are the limits? What would make it work better?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Promising to keep a secret. You can never promise a patient that a safeguarding concern stays confidential.
  • Investigating. Your job is to recognise and report concerns, not to question the family or decide what happened.
  • Forgetting to record. Always say what you would write down, when, and where.
  • Only talking about education. Health promotion is more than giving advice. Think about policy, community and the environment too.
  • Describing without evaluating. The PE criteria need you to judge how well something works.
  • Rushing. It is fine to pause and think before you answer.

A two-week prep plan

Week 1: knowledge

Re-read your practice safeguarding policy and find out who your safeguarding lead is. Revise the types and signs of abuse, the Care Act principles and how referrals work in your area. Then read the key chapters of Delivering Better Oral Health and your Unit 7 notes.

Week 2: practice out loud

Run every scenario on the practice page at least once, speaking your answers out loud. Ask a colleague or your supervisor to play the assessor and use the follow-up questions. Repeat any scenario where you ticked fewer than three quarters of the checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use notes?

Check with your tutor before your discussion. Practise without notes so you are ready either way.

What if I go blank?

Say so and ask for a moment. You can also ask the assessor to repeat or rephrase the question.

What happens if an AC isn't met?

Your assessor will give feedback on what was missing, and you will have the chance to cover it again.

Do I need to know legislation dates?

Knowing the names of the main laws and guidance helps, and the year is a bonus. Understanding what they mean for your practice is more important.
